HUGER expands Guangrao radial line to 2 million annual capacity.

A new Building Line takes annual all-steel radial capacity from 1.8M to 2M tyres. First containers cleared customs in Manaus on 12 May 2026.

Published 13 May 2026
By HUGER Press Desk
5 min read
Photo · HUGER Guangrao Plant · Building Line 6, commissioned April 2026

On the morning of 12 May 2026, the first containers of all-steel radial truck tyres rolled out of the brand-new Building Line 6 at HUGER's Guangrao plant — pushing the company's annual capacity from 1.8 million to a firm 2 million tyres. By sundown, those same containers had cleared customs in the port of Manaus, opening a new chapter in HUGER's Latin American programme that began only eighteen months ago.

The expansion is the culmination of a three-year capital programme totalling RMB 1.8 billion — covering a new mixing centre, two additional building lines, a re-equipped curing bay, and a fully automated X-ray inspection cell that brings 100% non-destructive testing in-house for the first time. None of it required a new factory: every piece of equipment was sited within the existing footprint of the Xishui Industrial Zone facility.

What the new line actually means

Capacity numbers are easy to publish and hard to read. To make the expansion concrete, here is what changed at line level:

  • +200,000 tyres / year. Building Line 6 alone contributes a sustained 555 tyres per shift, three shifts a day, six days a week — net after planned maintenance.
  • Same SKU mix, faster cadence. The line is dedicated to the FRIDERIC long-haul series (FA808, FA818, FA999) — exactly the three sizes that hit a six-week order backlog in Q1.
  • Casing-grade steel cord. All new output uses 4+9×0.22+0.15 high-tensile cord from our Liaocheng partner mill, the same specification that earned ECE R117 wet-grip class B in April.
  • Zero net headcount. 38 operator and inspection roles were created; 41 manual handling roles were re-skilled into them. Total plant headcount is unchanged at 1,920.

The Latin America corridor

The first 240 containers from the new line were pre-allocated to three distributors across Brazil, Peru, and Colombia. The opening shipment — 84 containers, FRIDERIC FA808 and FA999 in 12R22.5 — cleared customs in Manaus on the morning of 12 May. By tradition, our Latin American partner Transportes Continental photographed the first stack on the dock at 9:42 local time; that photograph is now the homepage of their fleet portal.

This makes Latin America HUGER's fourth-largest export region by volume, behind Southeast Asia, the Gulf, and West Africa. The corridor is now served by twenty-four-hour technical support from Sao Paulo and a parts depot in Lima that opened in February.

Why now

The honest answer is simple. Across 2025, the FRIDERIC long-haul book stayed sold-out for six consecutive months — partners in the Gulf and Latin America were quoting our regional dealers' inventory rather than ours. Building Line 6 is the first physical answer to that demand.

The capital was signed off in October 2024 by the parent group, 物产中大欧泰有限公司, on three conditions: no new land, no new headcount, no compromise on the in-line inspection regime that has earned the brand its CCC, GSO, SNI, DOT, ECE R117, INMETRO and SASO certifications. The plant team — led by deputy GM Liu Yang and chief engineer Wang Jianhua — delivered on all three.

Forty years of Guangrao tyres — but the only year that matters is the one in our X-ray lab.

Wang Jianhua · Head of Quality · HUGER Guangrao

What's next

Two follow-on projects are already in motion. A second X-ray cell, scheduled to come online in Q3, will let the plant inspect both shifts in parallel rather than sequentially. And the regional pattern line — pattern moulds 678 and 758 — receives a re-tooling round in August that will lift regional capacity by a further 90,000 tyres per year without further investment.

The longer arc remains unchanged: HUGER is — and intends to remain — an all-steel radial specialist. Two million tyres a year is not a destination. It is the new baseline.
